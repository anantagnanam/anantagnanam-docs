## Why Does the Sun Shine?

Deep inside the Sun, enormous pressure squeezes hydrogen gas together. This **nuclear fusion** releases an incredible amount of energy — the light and heat that reach us on Earth.

The Sun has been shining like this for about 4.6 billion years, and it will keep shining for billions more.

## The Gravity That Holds Everything

The Sun is also the **most massive** object in the Solar System — it contains about 99.8% of all the mass in the whole system. That enormous mass creates strong gravity, which gently pulls the planets toward it.

So why don't the planets fall into the Sun? Because they are also moving sideways, very fast. Gravity pulls them in; sideways motion carries them forward. Together, these two effects keep each planet looping around the Sun in a stable orbit.

<Callout type="tip" title="Try this picture">
Drop a marble into a spinning bowl and watch it roll around the rim. It keeps circling the center — not falling in, not flying out. That is a little like how planets orbit the Sun!
</Callout>

## The Scale of It All

<Callout type="warning" title="A truly humbling number">
About **1.3 million Earths** could fit inside the Sun. It is hard to imagine — and that is exactly the point. The Sun is a star, and stars are *enormous*.
</Callout>
