<Astra>
In the 1840s, astronomers noticed Uranus drifting from its predicted path. Something unseen was pulling on it. Two mathematicians — Urbain Le Verrier and John Couch Adams — calculated where that unseen world should be. When telescopes looked there, there it was: Neptune. It was the first planet discovered by prediction, before anyone laid eyes on it.

Neptune's deep blue, like Uranus's, comes from methane in its atmosphere.
</Astra>

## The Windiest World

Neptune is far from the Sun — so far that sunlight there is about 900 times dimmer than on Earth. Yet it is a place of furious activity. Its winds are the fastest measured in the Solar System, reaching more than 2,000 km per hour.

<Callout type="warning" title="Winds that would blow you away">
Neptune's winds can move faster than 2,000 km per hour — far faster than any wind on Earth. A gentle breeze on Neptune would be a ferocious hurricane here.
</Callout>

## A Moon That Goes Backward

Neptune's largest moon, **Triton**, orbits in the opposite direction to Neptune's spin — and no other large moon in the Solar System does that. Scientists think Triton is a captured object from the distant Kuiper Belt, snatched by Neptune's gravity long ago.

<DidYouKnow>
Triton is also one of the coldest places measured in the Solar System — about -235 °C — and it has geysers that spray nitrogen ice into its thin atmosphere.
</DidYouKnow>
