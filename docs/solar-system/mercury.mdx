## A Small, Speedy World

Mercury is the **smallest planet** in the Solar System — only slightly larger than our Moon. It has no moons of its own, and no rings.

Yet it is remarkably dense. Beneath its rocky surface lies an enormous iron core that takes up most of the planet — a hint that Mercury may have formed in a much hotter neighborhood.

## Day and Night, Extremes and All

Because Mercury has almost no atmosphere, there is nothing to spread heat around. The side facing the Sun can reach about 430 °C, while the side facing away can plunge to about -180 °C.

That is the biggest temperature swing of any planet in the Solar System.

## What Mercury Teaches Us

<DidYouKnow>
Mercury may be the fastest planet, but it also teaches us about slowness. Because it spins so slowly, the Sun appears to move backward across its sky near certain times of its long day!
</DidYouKnow>

Even a small, seemingly simple world has surprises. If a planet with almost no atmosphere can have such dramatic behavior, imagine what the bigger, more complex worlds hold.
