# Uranus

<StorySection>
Leela's book shows a planet that looks almost like a calm, pale blue-green ball — no stripes, no great storm, just a soft glow. Something looks odd about it, though. It is tipped over, rolling along its orbit like a ball being pushed sideways.

"Why is it lying down?" Leela giggles. "Is Uranus tired?"
</StorySection>

Why is Uranus blue-green? And why is it rolling on its side?
</Leela>

<Manjari>
For the color: think about a glass of water with a drop of greenish ink in it — what color does the light passing through become? For the tilt: imagine a spinning top that got bumped hard, long ago…
</Manjari>

<Astra>
Uranus gets its blue-green color from **methane** in its atmosphere. Methane absorbs red light, so the sunlight bouncing off Uranus returns to us tinted blue-green.

And the tilt? Long ago, something — likely a massive collision — knocked Uranus over. It now spins almost on its side, so its poles take turns facing the Sun for years at a time.
</Astra>

## Seasons That Last Decades

Because Uranus is tipped so far, its seasons are extreme — each one lasts about **21 Earth years**. One pole faces the Sun for a long summer while the other endures a long, dark winter.

It is also the coldest planet atmosphere in the Solar System, dipping to about -224 °C — even colder than Neptune, which is farther from the Sun.

## A Quiet World with Rings

Uranus has a faint system of rings and a family of 28 known moons. Its moons are named after characters from Shakespeare and Alexander Pope's poetry — a literary touch that makes Uranus unique among the planets.

<DidYouKnow>
Uranus is an "ice giant", like its neighbor Neptune. Ice giants are made largely of water, methane, and ammonia ices above a small rocky core — different from the gas giants Jupiter and Saturn, which are mostly hydrogen and helium.
</DidYouKnow>
