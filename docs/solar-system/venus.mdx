## Earth's Twin — With a Terrible Secret

Venus is almost the same size as Earth, which is why it is sometimes called Earth's twin. But the resemblance ends there.

Its thick atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide, and it traps heat so effectively that the surface simmers at about 465 °C — hot enough to melt lead. This is the **greenhouse effect** at its most extreme.

<Callout type="warning" title="The hottest planet">
Even though Mercury is closer to the Sun, Venus is hotter! Its blanket of clouds and carbon dioxide traps heat far better than Mercury's bare rock ever could.
</Callout>

## A World Turned Upside Down

Most planets spin one way, but Venus spins **backward** — the Sun would rise in the west and set in the east. No one knows exactly why, though a long-ago collision is one idea.

And the pressure at its surface is crushing — about 90 times the pressure on Earth, similar to being 900 meters deep in the ocean.

## Why Venus Matters to Us

<DidYouKnow>
Venus teaches us about our own planet. The same kind of greenhouse effect that bakes Venus exists on Earth too — but in a much milder form, thanks to our thin layer of heat-trapping gases. Understanding Venus helps scientists think about Earth's climate.
</DidYouKnow>
