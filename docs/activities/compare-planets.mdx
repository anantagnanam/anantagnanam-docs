## The Rocky Inner Worlds

Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars are the **inner planets** — smaller, rocky, and closer to the Sun. They have solid surfaces you could stand on (with a good spacesuit). Venus is the odd one out: similar in size to Earth, but with a crushing, scorching atmosphere.

## The Giant Outer Worlds

Jupiter and Saturn are **gas giants** — enormous balls of hydrogen and helium with no solid surface. Uranus and Neptune are **ice giants** — smaller, colder, and rich in water, methane, and ammonia ices.

<Callout type="warning" title="An unfair comparison">
The giants are so large that the four rocky planets could fit inside Jupiter alone, with room to spare. Size is the great divide of the Solar System!
</Callout>

## Compare Your Own Weight

The same mass feels different on different worlds, because gravity differs. On Mercury, you would weigh about 38% of your Earth weight. On Jupiter — if you could stand on it — you would weigh about 2.5 times more. On the Moon, only about one-sixth!
